Lucas Santos e Silva

I study auctions, mechanism design, and information economics, with applications to energy regulation and market design — work shaped by ten years designing and building electricity infrastructure before I turned to economics.

I am a PhD candidate in Economics at the Universidade de Brasília, currently completing a visiting research period in the Department of Systems Design Engineering at the University of Waterloo, under a Mitacs Globalink Research Award, working on Deep Electrification with Distributed Energy Resources.

My research sits at the intersection of microeconomic theory and applied microeconomics — game theory, auction design, mechanism design, and information economics — combined with empirical methods and causal inference. Much of my work applies these tools to public policy, regulation, and market design questions in electricity systems.

Before returning to academia, I spent a decade in the mining and infrastructure industry as an electrical engineer and project coordinator, with Vale, Anglo American, and SNC-Lavalin, and later served as an advisor at Brazil's Secretariat of Energy Planning and Development, Ministry of Mines and Energy (2022–2023). I hold an MSc in Economics (Universidade de Brasília, 2023), an MBA in Project Management (Fundação Dom Cabral), and a BSc in Electrical Engineering (Universidade Federal de Minas Gerais, 2007).
